# Chapter 1 — THE FIRST BREATH

| | |
|---|---|
| **Runtime** | 7:00 |
| **Character age** | 0 → 17 |
| **Theme** | Arrival without instructions; time as the only given |
| **Chapter question** | *"If no one gives you the manual, who writes it?"* |

---

## Characters in This Chapter

- **Claude:** infant, toddler (2), child (6), schoolboy (10), teenager (15–17). The left-eyebrow scar is established at age 6.
- **Amma:** mid-30s to 50. Loving, anxious, constantly asking, "Did you study?"
- **Appa:** late 30s to 50. Tired and proud. Talks about "settling down" and "a safe job".
- **Nana:** late 70s. Gentle, sparing with words. Gives Claude the watch.
- **Meera:** baby sister (introduced when Claude is 4) through age 13.
- **Teachers and classmates:** background faces that stand for society's voice.

## Storyboard

### Scene 1.1 — Darkness (0:00–0:35)

**Visual:** Total black for 6 seconds. Then the faintest red-orange glow, as if light is coming through skin. Abstract, out-of-focus shapes.
**Camera:** Locked. Extreme macro-style softness.
**Sound:** Silence → a single heartbeat at 60 bpm, then muffled womb ambience (low rumble, distant voices). The heartbeat motif is established.
**Motion graphics:** None. Pure image.

**NARRATION:**
> *(Silence over black. Then, over the heartbeat:)*
>
> Before you have a name… before you have a language… before you have a single opinion about the world… there is only this.
>
> *(beat)*
>
> A rhythm. Something keeping time.

---

### Scene 1.2 — The First Breath (0:35–1:15)

**Visual:** Hard cut to bright, overexposed white. A newborn's first cry (birth is suggested through hands, light and a towel, never shown graphically). The baby's eyes open, unfocused and wet. The ECU of the eye is held for 3 seconds.
**Camera:** Handheld for 2 seconds (the chaos of birth), then it settles to a slow push-in on the eye.
**Sound:** The cry, muffled hospital ambience, then everything drops away except the heartbeat.
**Motion graphics:** As the eye fills the frame, the pupil **match-cuts** into the centre of a thin warm-white circle in The Lens: the beginning of the lifespan timeline.

**NARRATION:**
> You arrive in this world without instructions.
>
> You don't choose your parents. You don't choose your birthplace, your circumstances, or your starting position.
>
> *(beat)*
>
> You are given something incredibly valuable: time.
>
> But nobody tells you how to use it.

---

### Scene 1.3 — The Lifespan Line (1:15–2:00)

**Visual:** The Lens. The circle unrolls into a horizontal line across the screen. Tick marks appear: 0, 10, 20 … 80. The line is then re-drawn as a **grid of 4,160 small dots**, one for every week of an 80-year life (52 × 80). A single dot glows ochre: "You are here — Week 1."
**Camera:** A slow virtual pull-back reveals the full grid.
**Motion graphics:**
- Label: `80 years ≈ 4,160 weeks` (small sans type)
- Dots softly fill in and fade grey as the narration moves on, showing time passing.
- Lower-third, very small: *"Average lifespans vary widely by country, era and circumstance. 80 is used for illustration."*

**Sound:** A soft synth pad enters under the heartbeat.

**NARRATION:**
> If you are fortunate, a human life lasts around four thousand weeks.
>
> It sounds like a lot. It isn't infinite. And here is the strange part: for the first several years, you won't even know you are spending them.

---

### Scene 1.4 — Learning to Walk (2:00–2:40)

**Visual:** Montage, warm afternoon light in a small apartment.
1. Infant on a woven mat, reaching for a light beam
2. Toddler gripping a sofa edge, letting go, falling, sitting stunned, then laughing
3. Toddler takes three steps toward Amma's outstretched hands
4. ECU: tiny feet on cool stone tiles

**Camera:** Low, at toddler eye level. A slider glides alongside the steps.
**Sound:** Real room tone, Amma's laughter, and the first entry of the main piano theme (3 notes only).
**Motion graphics:** None. Let it breathe.

**NARRATION:**
> Watch a child learn to walk. Nobody explains balance. Nobody gives a lecture on gravity.
>
> The child tries. Falls. Tries again. The child doesn't treat falling as a judgment. It's just information.
>
> *(beat)*
>
> It's one of the purest forms of learning a human will ever do, and most of us forget we were once that brave.

---

### Scene 1.5 — Language and First Emotions (2:40–3:25)

**Visual:**
- Age 2: Claude points at a crow on the window ledge. Nana says, "*Kaaka*." Claude repeats it badly, and both laugh.
- Age 4: Baby Meera arrives. Claude watches Amma hold her, and his face shows something new: jealousy.
- Age 6: He falls from a wall while showing off. Blood on his eyebrow, and Amma runs to him. **This is the scar.**
- Age 6, same evening: the scar is being cleaned, and he still laughs when Appa makes a face.

**Camera:** Each beat is a single static, painterly frame, with a slow 3% push-in.
**Motion graphics:** In The Lens, a simple **emotion wheel** sketches itself (joy, sadness, fear, anger, surprise, disgust). The segments light up as each emotion appears in the montage, then dissolve.

**NARRATION:**
> Then language arrives, and with it the ability to name things. To ask. To argue.
>
> Emotions arrive before you have words for them. Joy. Jealousy. Fear. Pain that has no logic to it.
>
> No one explains that these feelings are signals, not commands. You'll spend years learning that difference.

---

### Scene 1.6 — School and Society (3:25–4:30)

**Visual:**
- Age 10: rows of identical desks. A teacher writes on the board: *"Aim in life: ______."* Thirty children write "Doctor" or "Engineer". Claude's pen hovers. He writes "Engineer" because the boy next to him did.
- A report card. Appa reads the marks, nods without smiling and says, "Good. Next time, first rank."
- Age 15: tuition centre at night under fluorescent light. A poster on the wall reads "**SUCCESS = RANK**".
- Relatives at a wedding ask, "So what will you become?" Claude gives a rehearsed answer.

**Camera:** Symmetrical, centred framing on the classroom and tuition scenes to convey uniformity. Then a slow lateral dolly along rows of children doing the same thing.
**Sound:** School bell, scratching pens, the overlapping chatter of relatives. Muffled and slightly too loud, as if under water.
**Motion graphics:** Over the classroom, faint **thin arrows** start pointing at Claude from all directions, each labelled with an expectation: *Marks. Rank. Safe job. Don't embarrass us. Be like Sharma ji's son.* They thicken as the montage goes on.

**NARRATION:**
> Then comes school. And with school, something heavier: expectations.
>
> Not one voice. Many. Parents who love you and are afraid for you. Teachers with fifty students and one syllabus. Relatives who ask what you will become before you know who you are.
>
> *(beat)*
>
> Most of these voices mean well. But none of them can tell you what your life is for. Because they are still figuring out their own.

---

### Scene 1.7 — Fear of Failure (4:30–5:20)

**Visual:** Age 16, night. Claude sits at a desk with a mock-exam score in front of him: 54/100. Lamp light, the window dark. He hides the paper in a drawer. Then the bathroom mirror: he can't look at himself for long.
Cut to: the exam hall. His hand shakes slightly. The clock on the wall seems louder than it should be.
**Camera:** Handheld, close, slightly too tight. A slow rack focus from the clock to his face.
**Sound:** The heartbeat returns, faster at 90 bpm. The clock tick is exaggerated. The score drops out.
**Motion graphics:** A single word in serif type fades in and out of the darkness behind him: **FAILURE?** The question mark is important.

**NARRATION:**
> Somewhere around this age, a quiet fear moves in. The fear of failing. Of disappointing. Of being average.
>
> It's an understandable fear. When your worth seems measured in marks, every mistake feels like a verdict.
>
> But notice what happened to the child who used to fall and laugh. He didn't become less capable. He became more afraid of being seen falling.

---

### Scene 1.8 — The Watch (5:20–6:20)

**Visual:** Age 17. Nana's small room smells of camphor (suggested by an incense haze in the light). Nana is frail. He opens a tin box and takes out a steel wristwatch, scratched and still running. He puts it on Claude's wrist.

> **NANA:** "It doesn't tell you what to do with the time. Only how much is left."

Claude looks at it and doesn't fully understand.
Hard cut: weeks later, Nana's chair is empty. The watch is still ticking on Claude's wrist.
**Camera:** Two-shot at a respectful distance, then a slow push-in to the watch face. For the empty chair, one wide, locked-off frame held for 4 seconds.
**Sound:** Real ticking, with nothing else in the mix for 3 seconds. Then a single cello note.
**Motion graphics:** None.

**NARRATION:**
> Some lessons arrive as objects long before they arrive as understanding.
>
> *(beat — over the empty chair)*
>
> And some people leave before you have learned to ask them the right questions.

---

### Scene 1.9 — The Missing Manual (6:20–7:00)

**Visual:** The Lens. The 4,160-dot grid returns. About 900 dots are now grey (17 years). A thin outline of an **empty book** draws itself in the centre: the cover reads *"How to Live"* and every page is blank. The book closes.
Match cut: a real, blank black notebook on a desk, the same one Claude will pick up in Chapter 4. Claude's hand (age 17, wearing the watch) rests next to it.
**Camera:** Slow top-down push-in on the notebook.
**Sound:** Heartbeat and the 3-note theme, which stays unresolved. Dip to black.
**On-screen title (serif):** *Life doesn't come with an instruction manual.* → then, beneath: *You must consciously build one.*

**NARRATION:**
> By seventeen, you have absorbed thousands of rules. Rules about exams, about manners, about what others think.
>
> But no one has given you the one manual that matters: how to live a life you would choose, knowing it will end.
>
> *(beat)*
>
> That manual doesn't exist.
>
> So you'll have to write it yourself.

**→ Dip to black. Chapter card: "II — The First Battle."**

## Key Lessons

1. **No one receives instructions for life.** The rules you inherit (family, school, society) are other people's best guesses, shaped by their own fears and circumstances.
2. **Time is the primary resource.** An 80-year life is about 4,160 weeks. That's not a reason to panic, but it is a reason to pay attention.
3. **Failure was once natural.** Children learn by falling without shame. Much adult fear of failure is learned, which means it can be re-learned.
4. **Expectations are not purpose.** Advice can be loving and still not be right for you.
5. **Emotions are signals, not commands.** This idea is introduced here and developed in Chapter 2.

## Practical Exercises

1. **The Week Grid.** Draw (or print) a grid of 52 columns × 80 rows. Shade one box for every week you've already lived. Look at it for two minutes without judging. Write one sentence: *"What I notice is…"*
2. **The Inherited Rules Audit.** List 10 rules you live by (e.g. "A government job is the only safe job", "Never show weakness"). Beside each, write who taught it to you and whether you have ever tested it yourself. Mark each as *Keep / Question / Replace*.
3. **The Brave-Child Memory.** Recall something you learned as a child by repeatedly failing (cycling, swimming, a language). Write what made it feel safe to fail then. Ask what one condition from that time you could recreate now.
4. **Start the Manual.** Buy or find one notebook. On the first page, write only: *"Instructions for my life. Draft 1."* Leave the rest blank. You'll add to it in every chapter.

## Chapter Summary

Claude is born like every human: without a manual. The chapter follows him from his first breath through learning to walk, finding language, feeling his first emotions, and moving through school, expectations and the fear of failing. A 4,160-week lifespan grid shows how finite time is. His grandfather gives him a watch that measures time but can't say what to do with it. At seventeen, Claude has learned many rules but no way to live. The chapter ends on a blank notebook: **life doesn't come with an instruction manual, so you must consciously build one.**
