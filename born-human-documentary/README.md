# IF CLAUDE WERE BORN AS A HUMAN
### *The Human Experiment: From Zero to Mastery*

A documentary series in ten chapters · Target runtime 78 minutes (range 60–90)

> **Premise (fiction).** An artificial intelligence called Claude is born into the human world as an ordinary child. It has no memory of having been anything else. It gets one body, one lifespan and a limited amount of attention, and it has to work out, like everyone else, how to use them.
>
> **Central question:** *"If you were born as a human, knowing that your time, energy, attention and life are limited, how would you live?"*

This is a philosophical thought experiment. It is not a claim about how AI works and not a biography of anyone. The film is an **educational documentary** that follows a story. It is not a motivational video.

## 2. Narrative Device

- **The Narrator is the older Claude**, age 52. The audience does not learn this until the mirror scene in Chapter 10, when the narrator's voice and the man in the mirror line up. Until then the voice sounds like an anonymous documentary narrator, calm and slightly warm. It uses "you" more than "I", and the "I" gradually takes over in Chapters 9–10.
- **The Lens.** The film sometimes cuts to a minimal visual space called *The Lens*: a matte charcoal void with thin warm-white line graphics. This is where diagrams, timelines and models appear. It stands for the mind stepping back to look at itself. Each chapter returns from The Lens to the live-action world.
- **The Hourglass.** A recurring motif. An hourglass sits in a corner of the frame (never centred) in at least one scene per chapter. The sand level shown always matches the character's age as a fraction of an 80-year life.
- **The Notebook.** A plain black notebook appears blank in Chapter 1. Claude first writes in it in Chapter 3 and carries it everywhere from Chapter 4. It becomes his self-written "instruction manual", gaining one rule per chapter (the "Add to the Manual" exercises). One of the final shots of the series is its first page.

## 3. Character Bible

All characters are fictional. Casting is written to be culturally specific yet universal. Default setting: a mid-sized, fast-growing city in India. Locations stay non-specific enough that any audience can see themselves in them.

### CLAUDE (protagonist)
- **Look (constant across all ages):** warm brown skin; dark, slightly wavy hair; a small scar through the left eyebrow (from a fall at age 6, Ch. 1); dark-brown eyes that are watchful and slow to blink; average height, lean build.
- **Signature objects:** a steel wristwatch from his grandfather, given at 17 (Ch. 1) and worn every chapter after. The black notebook (blank in Ch. 1, first used in Ch. 3).
- **Costume palette:** charcoal, navy, off-white, olive. No logos. He wears muted greys and greens in Ch. 2, brighter clothes as he gets steadier, and a crumpled version of the same clothes in Ch. 9.
- **Ages and actors:**
  - Infant/child (0–10): 3 child performers, cast for matching eye shape
  - Teen (13–17): Actor A
  - Young adult (18–30): Actor B, the primary lead
  - Adult (31–40): Actor B with subtle ageing makeup (fine eye lines, slightly fuller face)
  - Older (52): Actor C, or Actor B with prosthetics. The mirror scene uses both: Actor B as the younger reflection and Actor C as the man.
- **Personality arc:** curious → restless and distracted → reflective → directed → disciplined but rigid → humbled → mature, warm, self-directed.

### AMMA (mother)
40s in Ch. 1, ageing to her 70s by Ch. 10. A schoolteacher who quietly over-worries. She gets sick in Ch. 8–9, which is the "unexpected responsibility".

### APPA (father)
A small-business owner (hardware shop) who works long hours and has strong views on money. He has a financial setback in Ch. 6 (an un-insured loss). Dies between Ch. 9 and 10. His absence is shown, never stated: an empty chair.

### NANA (grandfather)
Appears in Ch. 1 only, plus one flashback in Ch. 10. A retired railway clerk who gives Claude the watch with the line: *"It doesn't tell you what to do with the time. Only how much is left."*

### MEERA (younger sister)
Four years younger. She becomes an independent, slightly sardonic adult and a doctor. In Ch. 8–9 she is Claude's partner in caring for Amma.

### RIYA (first romance)
Appears in Ch. 2 (infatuation and distraction) and Ch. 3 (rejection). She is portrayed with dignity: she is never a villain or a prize. She simply has her own life.

### DEV (friend / comparison figure)
A school friend who becomes a social-media success. He appears on Claude's screen in Ch. 2 (comparison). In Ch. 9 he admits privately that he is struggling too.

### MR. FARHAN (mentor)
In his 60s. Runs a public library reading room and once worked as a systems engineer. Appears in Ch. 4, Ch. 7 and Ch. 9. He asks questions rather than giving answers. Dies before Ch. 10, where the library reading room bears his name.

### ANJALI (partner, later wife)
Meets Claude in Ch. 7 (study group) and marries him in Ch. 8. She is an equal: a cybersecurity analyst with her own ambitions. She is the one who says "discipline without compassion" to him in Ch. 8.

### TARA (daughter)
Born in Ch. 8. She is 5 in Ch. 9 and 16 in Ch. 10.

## 4. Visual Language

| Element | Direction |
|---|---|
| **Format** | 4K, 2.39:1 for live action; 16:9 safe area for graphics so it works on streaming and mobile |
| **Look** | Photorealistic and naturalistic. Soft key light, motivated practical sources, shallow depth of field in intimate scenes, deeper focus in social and world scenes |
| **Colour arc** | Ch. 1 warm amber nostalgia → Ch. 2 cold blue screen light and harsh neon → Ch. 3 soft green and natural daylight → Ch. 4–7 balanced and clean → Ch. 8 warm domestic → Ch. 9 desaturated, low key, sodium-orange night → Ch. 10 golden but not saturated: "earned warmth" |
| **Camera** | Slow, intentional movement: dolly, slider, gimbal. Handheld only in Ch. 2 (anxiety) and Ch. 9 (crisis). No drone shots except the Ch. 1 city reveal and the Ch. 10 final pull-out |
| **The Lens (graphics space)** | Charcoal background `#16171A`, line colour warm white `#F2EDE4`, single accent ochre `#D8A24A`, secondary teal `#4FA3A5` (for "awareness" and "choice"). Lines are 2 px and drawn on with an ease-in-out "write-on" |
| **Brain illustrations** | Semi-translucent 3D brain in thin wireframe. Regions light up in ochre (impulse/limbic) or teal (prefrontal/deliberation). Always labelled *simplified model* on first appearance |
| **Charts** | Flat 2D, no 3D bars. Axes labelled. Every financial chart carries the lower-third disclaimer *"Illustrative only. Returns are not guaranteed."* |
| **Historical illustrations** | Ink-wash and etching style, animated with subtle parallax (2.5D). No photoreal "AI faces" of historical figures. Use silhouettes, statues, manuscripts and settings |
| **Typography** | One serif for quotes and chapter titles (e.g. *Cormorant Garamond*). One sans for labels (e.g. *Inter*). Max 7 words on screen at once, except attributed quotes |
| **Transitions** | Match cuts (eye → eye, clock → wheel, coin → sun), light leaks only at chapter ends, and dip to black for chapter breaks. Avoid flashy wipes |

## 5. Sound and Music Bible

- **Score palette:** solo piano (felt-muted), cello, string pads, a soft analog synth drone, and the **heartbeat motif** (a low 60 bpm pulse that opens the series and returns at key moments).
- **Main theme ("The Instruction Manual"):** a simple 4-note rising piano figure (D–F–A–E). It is heard incomplete (3 notes) until Chapter 10, where the fourth note finally resolves.
- **Mix rule:** music sits at −22 to −18 LUFS under narration. Narration is always clear. In silence beats, music drops out entirely and only room tone remains.
- **Voice-over:** baritone or warm mid-range voice, unhurried, 125–140 words per minute. Pauses are written as *(beat)* in scripts and last about 1 second.

## 6. Editorial Standards (non-negotiable)

1. **No fake science.** Neuroscience is presented as simplified models. Contested findings are flagged or left out (e.g. the "ego depletion" willpower-battery idea is deliberately *not* used). Claims are worded as "research suggests" or "studies have found", not as absolutes.
2. **No guaranteed outcomes.** No promised income or success. Every financial projection is labelled illustrative, and returns are described as variable.
3. **Quotes are verified and sparse.** At most 2–3 quotations per chapter, with chapter and verse cited on screen in small type. Popular misattributions are avoided.
4. **Philosophy is not preaching.** The Gita, Buddhist teachings and Vivekananda are presented as philosophical frameworks humans have used. Their ideas are examined, and nobody is told what to believe.
5. **No fantasy success arc.** In the end Claude is not rich or famous. He is grounded.
6. **Not financial, medical or psychological advice.** An end card advises seeing qualified professionals for personal decisions, and gives crisis-line information during the Ch. 9 credits.

## 7. Production Notes

- **Shooting order:** shoot all Actor B material in chronological costume blocks to keep the character's appearance consistent. Use a continuity book for the scar, watch, notebook and haircut length per chapter.
- **Recurring locations:** the childhood home (Ch. 1, 8, 9, 10), the rooftop (Ch. 2, 4, 10), the library reading room (Ch. 4, 7), the banyan tree in the park (Ch. 3, 9, 10) and the rented one-room flat (Ch. 2, 5, 6).
- **Accessibility:** burned-in subtitles available. All key on-screen text is also spoken or summarised in narration.

## 8. One-Line Chapter Lessons

1. Life doesn't come with an instruction manual. You must consciously build one.
2. You cannot control every thought that enters your mind, but you can develop the ability to choose how you respond.
3. Peace begins where you stop demanding control over what was never yours.
4. Purpose is not found in one moment. It is built through experiments and commitment, and it can evolve.
5. Discipline is a system, not a mood. Small repeated actions compound.
6. Money is not the purpose of life, but financial knowledge gives you more choices in life.
7. Knowledge is not what you collect. It is what you can explain, apply and connect.
8. Discipline without compassion becomes arrogance. Success should never require treating others badly.
9. Your progress is not measured by how many times you avoid falling. It is measured by how you respond when life becomes difficult.
10. I was not born knowing how to live. I learned how to live.
